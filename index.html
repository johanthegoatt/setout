Setout

How many tiles you need, with spares, and where to start laying them so the wall doesn't finish on a thin sliver.

The surface

Measure the widest and longest points. A floor uses width and length; a wall uses width and height.

An opening you won't tile (a window, a door) comes off the area. The tiles cut around its edge are added back, since the offcut is usually too small to reuse. Same unit as the surface.

The tile

The pattern sets a baseline spare margin. Add more for a room full of corners, pipes or alcoves. Add the tile thickness and Setout works out the grout too, and from the tile size it recommends the trowel notch and the adhesive.

Why "where to start" matters

Laying full tiles flush from one wall is the easy way, but it leaves the whole leftover as a single cut against the far wall. If that comes out as a thin sliver it is fiddly to cut and the eye goes straight to it. The trade rule is to keep every cut as large as you can and never below about half a tile, so when a flush start would leave a sliver, Setout centres the field instead and splits the leftover across both ends. These are planning figures: always check them against the coverage printed on the tile box, and dry-lay a row before fixing anything.
